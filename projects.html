Predicting Retail Sales Channels: A Machine Learning Study

Comparing KNN and Naïve Bayes on multi-brand footwear retail data

Built classification models to predict whether a retail transaction was fulfilled Online, In-Store, or at an Outlet, using a combined 18,997-record dataset of Nike and Adidas US sales, then tested whether the model generalises across competing brands.

Orange Data Mining KNN Naïve Bayes

Cleaned and merged the Nike (9,360 rows) and Adidas (9,637 rows) datasets, then trained K-Nearest Neighbors (k = 10) and Naïve Bayes classifiers on price, volume, retailer, and region features in Orange Data Mining, evaluating performance via accuracy, F1, precision, recall, and ROC/AUC before testing cross-brand generalisation on Nike.

81% accuracy KNN outperformed Naïve Bayes (81.0% vs 67.1% accuracy, AUC 0.933 vs 0.850) and generalised almost identically to the Nike dataset (80.7%)

South Korea's Economic Growth: A Comparative Growth-Model Analysis

Solow, Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans & Overlapping Generations models — 1953–2024

Used Penn World Tables and World Bank data spanning 1953–2024 to trace South Korea's growth from the post-war years to its emergence as the world's 12th largest economy, modelling the trajectory through three competing growth frameworks — Solow, Ramsey–Cass–Koopmans (RCK), and Overlapping Generations (OLG).

R Stata 17 Penn World Tables

Estimated Solow phase diagrams and Barro convergence regressions (conditional and unconditional), derived the RCK saddle-path via a calibrated phase diagram, and modelled the OLG transition function to capture demographic effects on capital accumulation. Tested convergence, steady-state behaviour and dynamic efficiency (r ≥ n + g) across all three frameworks, and compared model fit against actual output and HDI data using log-linear specifications in R and Stata 17.

Dynamically efficient South Korea satisfies r > n + g throughout — RCK and OLG together best explain its post-1990 growth trajectory

Financial Market Anomalies and Uncertainty: The Friday Effect

An autoregressive (AR) time-series analysis across normal and volatile periods

Tested whether the "Friday effect" — the tendency for stock returns to run systematically higher on Fridays — holds up across changing market conditions, using an ARMA framework on daily index returns split into normal and volatile sub-periods.

Stata Power BI

Modelled log returns with ARMA(1,0) and ARMA(1,1) specifications incorporating Friday and volatility-regime dummy variables, using ADF unit-root tests, PACF/ACF diagnostics, and Wald chi-square tests to assess joint significance. Built a Power BI dashboard to visualise trading activity, volatility regimes, and market conditions alongside the regression results.

No Friday effect The Friday coefficient is statistically indistinguishable from zero (p ≈ 0.92) once AR(1) dynamics are accounted for — contradicting the classical weekend-effect anomaly

Stigler's Nutrition Problem: A Least-Cost Diet for Indian Women

A linear programming model built in GAMS, using Indian RDA standards

Applied George Stigler's classical Diet Problem to find the lowest-cost combination of common Indian foods that meets the Recommended Dietary Allowance for a moderately active 55kg woman, formulated as a linear program in GAMS.

GAMS Linear Programming

Converted nutrient and price data for 15 Indian food items into a nutrient-per-₹100 matrix, then built a linear program with 8 nutrient constraints (calories, protein, calcium, iron, iodine, vitamin C, niacin, B12) sourced from ICMR-NIN guidelines, solving in GAMS to minimise total daily food cost subject to those RDA constraints.

₹154.05/day Optimal cost of a nutritionally complete diet, dominated by millets with minor contributions from milk and leafy greens

Impact of CBDCs on Macroeconomic Variables

A panel data analysis of CBDC and emerging-CBDC economies, 2005–2025

Compared countries that have adopted central bank digital currencies (China, Nigeria, Bahamas, Jamaica) against emerging-CBDC economies (India, Brazil, South Africa, Uruguay) to assess how adoption affects inflation, GDP growth, money supply, and financial stability.

Stata

Ran pooled OLS with VIF diagnostics to rule out harmful multicollinearity, then applied random-effects panel regression — selected over fixed effects after a specification test failed to reject the null in both models — to separately estimate monetary-transmission and financial-stability effects across CBDC vs. emerging-CBDC countries.

RE model preferred Random effects selected for both the monetary-transmission and financial-stability models (p = 0.22 and p = 0.07), pointing to country-specific effects uncorrelated with the regressors
