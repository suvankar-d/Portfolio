Inflation–Unemployment Dynamics During the 2008 Global Financial Crisis

A comparative Phillips Curve analysis — United States vs. Germany

Examined the Phillips Curve relationship through the 2008 financial crisis using quarterly data from 2005–2011, comparing how inflation and unemployment interacted across the two economies.

Quarterly macroeconomic series for the US and Germany were modelled against the classical Phillips Curve specification to test whether the trade-off between inflation and unemployment held during the crisis window.

Weak & flat Inflation–unemployment relationship during the crisis, suggesting a flattening Phillips Curve

Democratic Destabilisation and Militarisation

A cross-country econometric analysis

Investigated how military expenditure, military personnel, the poverty gap and autocratic status influence a country's Democratic Index, using World Bank and OECD data.

A multiple regression model was built across countries, with VIF and Breusch–Pagan diagnostic tests used to confirm the model was free of harmful multicollinearity and heteroskedasticity.

R² = 0.58 of the variation in democratic stability explained by the model
